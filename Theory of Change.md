# Theory of Change

A theory of change is an explicit, testable account of how and why your actions should produce a desired change in a particular context. Use it to connect [[Goals]] and [[Strategy|strategy]] to execution, [[Metrics|measurement]], and [[Learning|learning]].

- [Map the causal path](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/the-analysis-function-theory-of-change-toolkit/) from **inputs → activities → outputs → outcomes → impact**.
  - Outputs are what you produce.
  - Outcomes and impact are the changes you expect those outputs to cause.
  - The theory lives in the arrows. A logic model shows what should happen, while a theory of change explains the mechanisms that make each transition happen.
- [Work backward from the desired long-term impact](https://www.theoryofchange.org/what-is-theory-of-change/) to identify the intermediate outcomes and conditions it requires, then choose interventions that can create them.
  - [Separate change theory from action theory](https://www.betterevaluation.org/frameworks-guides/managers-guide-evaluation/scope-evaluation/describe-theory-change). Explain how change happens, then how your intervention will activate it.
- [Make every causal assumption explicit](https://www.betterevaluation.org/frameworks-guides/managers-guide-evaluation/scope-evaluation/describe-theory-change), including supporting evidence, dependencies, context, risks, and unintended outcomes.
- [Treat the model as a living hypothesis](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240097063/).
  - Attach [[Metrics|indicators]] to each step, test where the chain breaks through [[Experimentation|evaluation]], and revise it as evidence or context changes.
  - Focus evidence collection on [causal hotspots](https://ieg.worldbankgroup.org/evaluations/quality-guidance-contribution-analysis-practice/quality-guidance-contribution-analysis). Prioritize links that are important, uncertain, or contested. Seek both confirming and disconfirming evidence.
  - [Distinguish theory failure, implementation failure, and methodology failure](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/documents/evaluation/set-up-and-recruitment/EEF-Theory-of-Change-and-logic-model-guidance-September-2022.pdf?v=1663931550) when results disappoint.
- Keep the model proportional to the intervention.
  - Linear chains suit simple cases. In [[Systems|complex systems]], include feedback loops, external factors, and multiple pathways to impact.
  - [Claim contribution rather than sole attribution](https://www.betterevaluation.org/methods-approaches/approaches/contribution-analysis). Outcomes usually result from a causal package of actors and conditions, so test rival explanations and state the remaining uncertainty.
- [Treat it as a participatory thinking process, not a compliance artifact](https://gsdrc.org/document-library/review-of-the-use-of-theory-of-change-in-international-development/). Ask whose desired change and assumptions it represents. Power shapes which perspectives are heard and which narratives go unchallenged.
